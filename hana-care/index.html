Our Difference

Care Rooted in Culture

Many seniors who immigrated to the United States built lives bridging two worlds. As they age — and especially as memory changes — the anchors of home culture become not just comfort, but essential to wellbeing.

言

Language

For many seniors, English was a second language adopted by necessity. As cognitive decline progresses, Japanese often reemerges as the primary language of thought, memory, and emotion.

Our caregivers speak Japanese — not as a novelty, but as a clinical and human necessity. Familiar words reduce anxiety, enable accurate health communication, and maintain dignity.

食

Food & Nourishment

Washoku — traditional Japanese cuisine — is more than nutrition. Miso, rice, pickled vegetables, and familiar flavors connect seniors to memory, family, and identity.

Our care programs support culturally appropriate meal planning and preparation. We understand dietary preferences within Japanese culinary traditions and work with clients and families to maintain the meals that feel like home.

湯

Bathing & Ritual

The ofuro — a deep soaking bath — is a cornerstone of Japanese daily life. It is not merely hygiene; it is relaxation, reflection, and tradition. For seniors accustomed to this ritual, a perfunctory shower can feel disorienting and undignified.

Where possible, our care model supports and accommodates traditional bathing preferences as part of a holistic daily routine — a small thing that makes an enormous difference.

縁

Community & Connection

Isolation is one of the greatest risks in senior care. Cultural disconnection compounds it. We work to connect seniors with Oregon's Japanese-American community, cultural events, and peer groups who share their background and values.

From ikebana and shodo to cultural celebrations, shared meals, and familiar media — belonging matters, at every age.

The Care Continuum

Meeting You at Every Stage

Needs change over time. Hana Care can grow with you — from light support at home to full-time skilled care and, when the time comes, compassionate end-of-life services.

01

Independent with Support

Light in-home help — a few hours a week. Companionship, errands, meal preparation, language support, and cultural connection while the senior remains fully at home.

02

Daily Living Assistance

Regular personal care — bathing, dressing, medication reminders, and mobility support. Care is shaped around familiar cultural routines, not against them.

03

Assisted Living

Transition to a residential setting with around-the-clock support. We help identify, evaluate, and navigate placement in communities with experience serving culturally diverse seniors.

04

Memory & Skilled Nursing Care

Specialized care for dementia, stroke recovery, and other complex medical needs — with Japanese-language-capable staff at the center of the care plan.

05

Hospice & End-of-Life

Comfort-focused care that respects cultural, spiritual, and family traditions. Families are guided and supported with sensitivity through every step of this final chapter.

Our Mission

Care Should Feel Like Home

Hana Care was founded on a simple belief: that aging with dignity means aging as yourself. For seniors who came to the United States from Japan, that means holding onto the language, food, rituals, and customs that shaped their lives — even as care needs grow.

We serve seniors of all backgrounds across Oregon. Our team brings deep expertise in Japanese cultural traditions, Japanese-language communication, and the unique challenges facing immigrant seniors and their families navigating the American care system.

We are proud to be an inclusive, welcoming provider. Our cultural programs are available to all clients who wish to participate. We provide services without regard to race, national origin, religion, or any characteristic protected by law.
